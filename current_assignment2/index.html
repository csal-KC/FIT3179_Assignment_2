The Great Barrier Reef: How is it Changing?

Explore where reefs are monitored, how coral cover varies, and where crown-of-thorns starfish are detected. Then examine a recent coral-cover trend.

01 | THE EVIDENCE

First, where do we have observations?

Dots show reefs included in two AIMS monitoring methods. A reef may appear in both datasets. These are surveyed locations, not every reef in the Great Barrier Reef.

03 | POTENTIAL PRESSURE

Where were crown-of-thorns starfish (COTS) detected?

Each 1° x 1° cell shows the percentage of surveyed reefs with COTS detected in 2023. This measures detection, not starfish abundance or coral loss. Hover to check sample sizes, especially for cells with few surveyed reefs.

Darker cells indicate a higher detection percentage among surveyed reefs.

04 | A MORE RECENT VIEW

What does the 2025 monitoring series suggest?

This line shows the unweighted mean modelled coral cover across 51 consistently observed MMP series from 2021 to 2025. MMP uses different methods and locations from the LTMP maps. The trend is not a reef-wide estimate.

05-06 | PATTERNS AND VARIATION

How much does coral cover differ?

Compare sector-year averages, then inspect variation across shelf positions. Grey cells indicate sector-years with fewer than three sampled reefs or no observations.

The boxplot shows the median, middle half of reefs, whiskers and outliers. Shelf codes are I (inshore), M (mid-shelf), O (outer shelf).

07-08 | ASSOCIATIONS AND COMPOSITION

What else is observed on surveyed reefs?

Explore whether COTS counts and coral cover vary together. This is an association, not evidence of causation.

Compare the average benthic composition of sampled reef sites. Categories are normalised to sum to 100% within each shelf.

09-10 | TIME AND REGIONAL CONTEXT

How do detections and coral cover compare?

COTS detection rates show the share of surveyed reefs with a positive observation. Different years include different surveyed reefs.

In a separate MMP dataset, compare modelled hard coral cover across named reef zones in 2025. Do not directly compare these values with LTMP estimates.

11-13 | DISTRIBUTIONS AND MATCHED REEFS

How is coral cover changing across reefs?

Yearly distributions show the range of reef-level coral cover. The sampled reefs can differ by year, so these are not matched trends.

To compare the same reefs, the next chart includes only reefs sampled in both 2022 and 2023. Each line is one reef; green highlights increases of at least 10 percentage points.

Finally, compare the largest measured increases and decreases. These are changes in coral cover, not evidence of what caused them.
