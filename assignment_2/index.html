FIT3179 | DATA VISUALISATION 2

The Great Barrier Reef: How is it Changing?

A geographic journey through monitoring coverage, hard coral condition and crown-of-thorns observations. Followed by a newer coral-cover series.

01 | THE EVIDENCE

First, where do we have observations?

Monitoring is not evenly distributed across the Great Barrier Reef. The dots identify reefs appearing in either of the two AIMS Long-Term Monitoring Program (LTMP) survey datasets. Colours distinguish the survey methods; a reef may appear in both. These are monitoring locations, not a census of every reef.

02 | CONDITIONS

Hard coral cover differs between surveyed reefs

Circle size represents the mean percentage of hard coral cover across available LTMP site-transect observations at each reef during the 2022-23 survey year. Larger circles indicate more hard coral cover, not larger reefs. Only reefs sampled in this survey year appear.

03 | POTENTIAL PRESSURE

Where were crown-of-thorns starfish (COTS) detected?

Each 1 degree x 1 degree (latitude x longitude) geographic cell shows the percentage of reefs surveyed in 2023 with a positive crown-of-thorns observation in the LTMP manta-tow dataset. This is detection among surveyed reefs, not starfish abundance or proof of coral loss. Hover to inspect sample sizes; cells with few reefs are less stable.

04 | A MORE RECENT VIEW

What does the 2025 monitoring series suggest?

The Marine Monitoring Program (MMP) uses a different method and geography. This line shows the unweighted mean of modelled hard coral cover across the same 51 domain-zone-depth series observed in every year from 2021 to 2025. It is not directly comparable to the LTMP maps and is not a reef-wide estimate.

Data and Interpretation Notes

Data Sources: The data used in this visualisation comes from the Australian Institute of Marine Science (AIMS), including the Long-Term Monitoring Program (LTMP) and Marine Monitoring Program (MMP).
